Worked example

One service. Three jurisdictions.

Who can compel access to the data?

A website on a .uk domain, run by a US company, with its data stored in India. Each of those facts brings its own laws, and its own agencies able to use them. An assessment that looks at only one jurisdiction misses the other two.

The three
jurisdictions

Each layer brings its own powers.

Domain · .uk

United Kingdom

A .uk domain suggests UK registration. That is the first legal touchpoint, wherever the site is hosted or managed.

Agencies with legal authority

  • GCHQ Government Communications Headquarters
  • MI5 Security Service
  • Home Office
  • National Crime Agency

Powers

  • Investigatory Powers Act 2016High

    Gives GCHQ and MI5 broad data access powers, including technical capability notices.

Corporate control · US

United States

The company running the service is American. That brings US jurisdiction through corporate control, wherever the servers are.

Agencies with legal authority

  • FBI Federal Bureau of Investigation
  • NSA National Security Agency
  • DOJ Department of Justice
  • FISC Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court
  • DHS Department of Homeland Security

Powers

  • CLOUD ActHigh

    Lets the DOJ and FBI compel US companies to provide data, wherever it is stored.

  • National Security LettersHigh

    Let the FBI demand data in secret, with gag orders that limit transparency.

  • Executive Order 12333Medium

    Authorises NSA signals intelligence collection outside the US, with minimal oversight.

Storage · India

India

The data is stored in India. That adds a third jurisdiction with its own access powers, whoever controls the company.

Agencies with legal authority

  • CERT-In Indian Computer Emergency Response Team
  • Intelligence Bureau
  • NATGRID National Intelligence Grid
  • Reserve Bank of India
  • Ministry of Electronics and IT

Powers

  • Information Technology Act 2000High

    Section 69 lets the government direct authorised agencies, including the Intelligence Bureau, to intercept, monitor and decrypt information.

  • Payment data localisationMedium

    The Reserve Bank of India requires payment system data to be stored only in India.

  • Telecommunications interceptionMedium

    NATGRID can access telecommunications data with authorisation from the Home Secretary.

How they
interact

Together, they reach further than apart.

These jurisdictions don't operate in isolation. They interact in ways that can amplify risk.

UK and US

Five Eyes intelligence sharing High

The Five Eyes arrangement lets agencies including the NSA, GCHQ and Canada's CSE share intelligence across borders.

US and India

Corporate control High

The DOJ can compel the US parent company to access data held in India, bypassing local jurisdiction.

All three

Jurisdictional conflict Medium

Competing claims between UK, US and Indian agencies create legal uncertainty about whose demand takes precedence.

Why it matters

One jurisdiction is never the whole picture.

Assessing a cloud service against a single jurisdiction misses where most of the risk comes from. GLARS is built for the whole picture.

Every layer

The domain, the company in control and the physical hosting location are each assessed.

Interactions

Cooperation agreements and legal conflicts change the score, not just the worst single jurisdiction.

One line to share

Vector notation states every factor precisely, so the assessment can be passed on and checked.

A number to compare

A judgement that was subjective becomes a score two options can be measured against.

Decisions, not just ratings

The parts of the score point to what to change in architecture or contracts.
