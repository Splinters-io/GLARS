What GLARS is

Geo-Legal Access Risk Score

GLARS is an open methodology for scoring how exposed your data is to government access, embargoes and sanctions, based on where it is stored and who controls it.

It rates seven components using published law rather than politics, and combines them into one score from 0 to 100 with a Low, Medium or High risk level, so hosting and provider choices can be compared like for like. Read the full methodology

How to use the calculator

  1. 1

    Decide what you’re assessing

    List the jurisdictions that can reach your data: where it is stored, where the provider’s parent company is based, and where the domain is registered. Assessing several together? Give each component the highest score among them.

    Assessing a domain or IP address? Juri works these out for you.

  2. 2

    Score the seven components

    Set each from 0 to 100, where higher always means more risk:

    0
    No such power or restriction
    50
    Exists, but limited or overseen
    100
    Broad and unchecked

    Open “What this measures” under any component for the factors to weigh.

  3. 3

    Read the result

    You get a score out of 100, a Low, Medium or High risk level, what set the score, and the controls recommended for that level.

  4. 4

    Save or share

    Copy the vector to record the assessment, or copy a link that reopens the calculator with the same scores.

New to GLARS? Pick a worked example at the top of the calculator to see a completed assessment and the reasoning behind each score, then change it to fit your case.

    Step 2 Core legal components

    These five are weighted and added together to make the base score.

    Weight 20%

    Higher means weaker judicial control over government access.

    What this measures

    The strength and independence of judicial review governing government access to data.

    • Prior judicial authorisation before access
    • Independence of courts from the executive
    • Specificity and narrowness of access requests
    • Availability of meaningful appeal
    Judicial Oversight in the Deep Dive
    Weight 25%

    Higher means broader legal powers to access data.

    What this measures

    The breadth and depth of legal authority granted to government agencies to access data.

    • Scope of authorised collection
    • Bulk versus targeted collection
    • Powers to compel service providers
    • Claimed authority beyond borders
    Agency Powers in the Deep Dive
    Weight 15%

    Higher means more mandated access capabilities.

    What this measures

    Technical capabilities providers must build or maintain to facilitate government access.

    • Mandated access mechanisms
    • Key escrow or decryption obligations
    • Mandatory data retention
    • Powers to compel infrastructure changes
    Technical Requirements in the Deep Dive
    Weight 25%

    Higher means greater legal reach beyond the jurisdiction’s borders.

    What this measures

    How far the jurisdiction’s laws assert authority over data and providers outside its territory.

    • Claims over data stored abroad
    • Claims based on provider nationality
    • Claims based on data-subject nationality
    • Claims based on corporate control
    Extraterritoriality in the Deep Dive
    Weight 15%

    Higher means less visibility into government access.

    What this measures

    How visible government data-access activity is to the public, providers and affected people.

    • Public reporting of access requests
    • Notification of affected individuals
    • Prevalence of gag orders
    • Availability of meaningful statistics

    The severity of trade restrictions that prohibit or limit commercial activity with a country.

    What’s next: five proposed enhancements
    
            
    Today’s score is a static, country-level baseline. These proposals would make it reflect context and change over time. None of them is part of the score yet, and because several are multipliers, how to keep results within 0–100 is still an open question.
    
            
      
                
    1. 
                    1
                    
      Temporal intelligence
      
                    
      Adjust for change over time: how fast relevant law is moving, which way enforcement is trending, and how likely an abrupt legal shift is.
      
                
      2. 
                
    2. 
                    2
                    
      Industry context
      
                    
      Apply sector multipliers to specific components, for example healthcare 1.2–1.5×, financial services 1.3–1.6×, critical infrastructure 1.4–1.7×.
      
                
      3. 
                
    3. 
                    3
                    
      Enforcement history
      
                    
      Calibrate written law against how it is used: focus on foreign entities, enforcement abroad, penalties imposed, and how often appeals succeed.
      
                
      4. 
                
    4. 
                    4
                    
      Data sensitivity
      
                    
      Weight the score by the data at stake, from public material (0.8–1.0×) to critical data such as biometrics or national-security information (2.0–2.5×).
      
                
      5. 
                
    5. 
                    5
                    
      Jurisdiction interactions
      
                    
      Replace “take the highest” with how jurisdictions combine. Cooperation such as the UK-US Data Access Agreement amplifies risk; conflicts and blocking laws such as GDPR Article 48 or the EU Data Act can reduce it.

    An Objective, Apolitical Framework
    
                
    GLARS is designed as a neutral, apolitical assessment tool that relies solely on documented legal frameworks, published government powers, and verifiable technical requirements across jurisdictions.
    
                
                
    
                    
    
                        
    Evidence-Based Assessment
    
                        
    GLARS evaluations are based strictly on publicly available information about legal frameworks, government access powers, and technical mandates. The framework uses primary sources like legislation, court decisions, and government publications.
    
                    
    
                    
                    
    
                        
    Objective Scoring
    
                        
    Risk scores are calculated using consistent criteria applied uniformly across all jurisdictions. The methodology assesses the presence or absence of specific legal powers and mechanisms, not subjective judgments about governments or political systems.
    
                    
    
                    
                    
    
                        
    Transparent Methodology
    
                        
    The GLARS assessment approach is fully documented and transparent. Users can trace how each score is derived, which legal frameworks contribute to the evaluation, and the weighting applied to different factors.
    
                    
    
                    
                    
    
                        
    Practical Utility
    
                        
    The framework focuses on practical risk assessment for data governance, helping organizations understand the specific legal powers that could impact their data regardless of geopolitical considerations or national preferences.
