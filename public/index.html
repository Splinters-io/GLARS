Before the data moves

Every location comes with laws.

Which ones can reach your data?

Data answers to the law of wherever it is stored, whoever controls the service, and whichever domain it sits under. Each of those can give a government the power to compel access.

Arguments about where data may sit are usually settled on instinct. One country feels risky, another feels fine, and neither position is written down anywhere it could be challenged.

Read the method ↗ See it in a worked example ↗

An open methodology. Built only from primary sources: legislation, court decisions and published government powers.

What GLARS gives you

A score. And the reasons behind it.

GLARS scores the legal, embargo and sanctions risk of holding data in a jurisdiction, or moving it through one. The same criteria are applied everywhere, so two jurisdictions can actually be compared.

The score breaks into its parts: legal frameworks, embargoes, sanctions and government access powers. You can see what drove the number, not just the number.

The principle

What a government can compel. Not what anyone thinks of it.

GLARS is deliberately apolitical. It relies only on documented legal frameworks, published government powers and verifiable technical requirements. What comes out is an assessment somebody can disagree with on the evidence, which is more than instinct ever offered.

Evidence

Primary sources only

Legislation, court decisions and government publications. Nothing that is not publicly documented goes into a score.

Consistency

The same criteria everywhere

Each jurisdiction is assessed on the presence or absence of specific legal powers and mechanisms, not on judgements about its government or political system.

Transparency

Every score can be traced

The method is fully documented: how each score is derived, which legal frameworks contribute and how each factor is weighted.

Use

The powers that touch your data

GLARS is for data governance decisions. It shows the specific legal powers that could affect your data, whatever the geopolitics.

Why it exists

The score is the start of the conversation.

While the Risk Score might be important to some, the true value of GLARS lies in ensuring that essential questions have answers, and that decision-makers understand the material effects on data custodians, data owners, and those accountable for compliance. It's about ensuring full consideration of data location risks, so informed decisions can be made with a complete picture of jurisdictional implications.
